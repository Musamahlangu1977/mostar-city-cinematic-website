01 / THE CROSSING

One arch.
A thousand stories.

1566 · Rebuilt 2004

Designed by Hayruddin, the Old Bridge joined the banks of the Neretva in 1566. Destroyed in 1993 and rebuilt in 2004, its pale stone arch is a powerful symbol of renewal.

UNESCO ↗

02 / THE OLD BAZAAR

Follow the
copper lanes.

Ottoman old town · Living craft

Beside the bridge, narrow stone lanes bring the old trading quarter close. Shopfronts, copperwork and coffee tables turn the city’s Ottoman heritage into an everyday street scene.

Old town history · UNESCO ↗

03 / ABOVE THE RIVER

A quieter
point of view.

Early 17th century · Ottoman architecture

A dome, a slender minaret and a riverside courtyard: the mosque is part of Mostar’s early 17th-century skyline. From this stretch of the east bank, the bridge and rooftops unfold together.

Architectural record · MIT ↗

04 / BEHIND THE WALLS

The city,
at home.

Ottoman domestic heritage

Beyond the monuments lies the scale of everyday life. Kajtaz House preserves a family dwelling from Mostar’s Ottoman past, offering a more intimate thread through the city’s architectural story.

Conservation history · World Monuments Fund ↗

05 / REMEMBERING MOSTAR

Pause.
Look closer.

1992–1995 · Lives behind the landmarks

The Halebija tower guards the western approach to Stari Most. The city’s war-photography exhibitions offer another perspective: records of civilian life, loss and survival during the 1992–1995 conflict.

Explore Mostar’s war-photography history ↗

06 / THE WHOLE PICTURE

A city held
by a river.

UNESCO World Heritage · Since 2005

Step back and the pieces connect: the bridge, minarets, stone roofs and green water. Mostar’s Old Bridge Area entered the World Heritage List in 2005, recognising a city shaped by the meeting of cultures.

World Heritage listing · UNESCO ↗
